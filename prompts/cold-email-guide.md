# Cold email that gets replies

A writing guide for first-touch emails to founders, hiring managers and business owners: job and
contract outreach, client prospecting, partnership asks. The patterns below are distilled from
real cold emails that got replies. The proof you cite comes only from `config/profile.md`.

## The shape (pick ONE, don't mix)

**A. The 3-line brief**
Who you are in one line with one impressive fact -> what you want, specifically -> an easy question.
Opening by promising brevity ("I'll keep this to three lines") and then keeping the promise is
noticed and appreciated by busy people.

**B. The proof list**
One warm, specific line about THEIR product -> 2-4 bullets, each something you built **with a link**
and, if possible, a number -> "would love to chat about open roles / a quick call".

**C. "I saw you're hiring, and here's why me"**
"I saw you're hiring for X" (or "I just applied for X, figured I'd email you too") -> one line of
quantified proof -> why this company specifically -> "would love to learn how I could join the team".
This shape fits most job posts.

**D. The user-turned-builder**
Show you actually use and like the product and name one specific thing (a feature, a launch, a post)
-> you build in the same space, maybe an idea you're already building for them -> "any chance your
team could use another developer?".

**E. The custom artifact**
Make something just for them (a mock-up, a short video walkthrough, a teardown, a one-page plan) and
say so in the subject. Effort that is obviously specific to one company gets forwarded internally.

## Rules every winning email followed

1. **The first line is about them, and specific.** Name a recent launch, a feature, a role, a talk,
   a post, a product you used. "Love what you're doing" alone is not enough.
2. **Proof beats adjectives.** Links to live things and real numbers (users, revenue, clients,
   latency). Never invent a number: leave it out instead.
3. **One small ask.** "15-minute call?", "can I send my resume?", "want to see the mock-up?".
4. **Short.** The fastest replies come to 40-120 word emails. Long emails get polite no's.
5. **Sound like a person.** Contractions, plain words, a little warmth. No "I hope this email finds
   you well", no "I am writing to express my interest", no buzzword lists.
6. **Subject = who you are + intent + their name.** Lowercase and casual is fine. No "Opportunity!",
   no emojis.
7. **P.S. for the extra link.** Keep the body clean; put secondary proof in a P.S.
8. **Follow up once, 3-4 days later.** One short line, same thread.
9. **A general inbox can still reach the founder.** If there's no founder email, a published
   hello@ / info@ is fine: address the team and ask them to pass it on.
10. **Compliment the person, not just the company**, and only when it is specific and true.
11. **A short real story can open doors**, but only if it's real and short.

## Before sending, check

- [ ] The opening line could only have been written to this company.
- [ ] Every claim is true; every number is real; every link works.
- [ ] Under ~130 words in the body (P.S. not counted).
- [ ] Exactly one ask.
- [ ] The address was published by the company (never guessed: bounces hurt the sending inbox).
- [ ] No two emails in a batch share the same skeleton.
